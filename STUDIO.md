## V2 differentiator, task and primary action

**Differentiator:** “Make it your day” turns a local dealer page into one ownership day:
encounter, ignition, ride, useful details, personal finish, purchase conversation,
handover, care and a Kaduwela destination. **Visitor task:** decide whether the Dio 125
fits daily life and choose the right branch conversation. **Primary action:** call Macro
at the public Maps number. The page makes no pricing, stock, finance or service promise.

## V2 signature scene contract

- **Initial:** a close, unlit red Dio portrait fills the viewport while one proposition,
  one short lede and `Talk to Macro` establish the visit.
- **Interaction:** progress within the hero alone expands an illuminated product image
  from the headlamp, adds a brief light response and resolves the model statement. The
  remaining page cuts deliberately through local ride, official feature, colour,
  ownership, handover and care scenes; no cutout is presented as simulated driving.
- **Resolved:** the hero holds the illuminated full-product portrait and `Silent start.
  A whole city ahead`, then hands off to the real-world ride chapter.
- **Persists/transforms:** the same red/black Dio campaign identity and Macro action
  persist. Framing, illumination, environment, feature subject and visitor decision
  transform between beats.
- **Reverse scroll:** every hero property derives from clamped progress and returns to the
  exact opening state. Colour and ownership selections change only through direct controls.
- **Reduced motion/fallback:** use the resolved reveal still with the opening proposition;
  remove scrub, parallax and spatial entrances. Since the page needs no WebGL or canvas,
  the semantic image/HTML experience is also the runtime fallback.

## V2 composition diff against the last three catalog sites

- **SHIFT Workspaces:** SHIFT used a fixed architectural threshold and one comparison
  variable. V2 is a linear ownership-day campaign whose only sticky sequence is the
  ignition; focal subject is a physical scooter moving through human contexts.
- **Apex Hybrid:** Apex used editorial overlays on one persistent retail environment. V2
  changes section grammar—full-bleed street, split official details, interactive red
  colour studio, asymmetric intent editorial, handover, care—and uses product desire as
  the spine.
- **Kinetic House:** Kinetic decomposed a speaker through component motion. V2 keeps the
  scooter whole and credible, using photographic cuts, one light-origin vector reveal and
  direct selection rather than exploded construction.

## Differentiator, visitor task and action

**Differentiator:** an official local Honda sales-and-care relationship that can take a
rider from an old bike to a new one quickly, then support the bike after handover.

**Primary visitor task:** decide which current ride fits, understand the next financial
step and start a human conversation without searching a Facebook feed.

**Primary action:** `Talk to Macro` via the customer-confirmed WhatsApp number. Secondary
actions: `See current rides`, `Start a trade-in`, `Book service`, `Call` and `Directions`.
The prototype may demonstrate these paths with safe local UI; it will not submit data or
claim a live booking without an authorized backend.

The content system should support English and Sinhala because the existing feed already
uses English display phrases with Sinhala sales/service explanations. Both languages need
real written copy; the switch must not expose machine-placeholder text.

## Direction: One road, three decisions

Working proposition: **Your next ride, one road away.** Supporting sequence:
**Choose → Trade → Care.** This grows directly from the current trade-in posts, quick
leasing/handover review, service-centre presence and `Driven by trust, built for you`.

The signature experience follows one Honda scooter along a Kaduwela road rather than
dropping different bikes into separate cards. The road and bike persist while the world
changes around them:

1. **Choose:** low, three-quarter scooter portrait; scroll advances the road and swaps
   authentic model/color states while one compact fact changes at a time.
2. **Trade:** the centre line becomes a transparent old-ride → assessment → payment →
   handover route. The customer's existing bike recedes in the mirror while the selected
   Honda remains ahead. This is explanatory choreography, not a loan promise.
3. **Care:** the road crosses the showroom threshold and becomes a red service-bay guide.
   Genuine-parts and after-sales details attach to relevant areas of the same scooter,
   then the scene resolves at the branch with WhatsApp, call and directions.

The bike is the persistent focal subject, the road line is the navigation/progress
language, and the branch is the destination. Red belongs to Honda/Macro; cobalt road sky,
warm sodium light and sharp yellow route marks bring the energetic local sales voice
without recreating the crowded social graphics.

## Candidate comparison

### A — accurate live 3D configurator

Highest interaction ambition: a licensed Dio model with real materials, color switching,
camera travel and hotspots. It could carry the whole site, but no accurate licensed GLB
or authoring source is available from the supplied links. Procedurally faking the Honda
silhouette would damage product trust. Keep this route only if the customer/Honda supplies
a usable model or a verified authoring path appears.

### B — authored product photography / short frame sequence plus DOM and Canvas road

Chosen pilot route. A clean cutout or locally rendered/licensed product sequence can
deliver SONIQ-like material quality, while DOM type and a code-authored road/service line
connect decisions. It is reversible, mobile-composable and zero-billing once source
imagery exists. It can later be upgraded to 3D without changing the narrative.

### C — bright social-campaign grid

Cheapest route and closest to the current feed, but it would preserve the business's
main digital limitation: every promotion becomes a separate poster. Use its color and
local directness as input, not its page structure.

## Composition diff against the previous three catalog sites

### Versus SHIFT Workspaces

- **Page structure:** SHIFT is a room → threshold → resolved environment followed by one
  yellow action panel. Honda is a road journey with decisions embedded along the route
  and a branch destination.
- **Motion grammar:** SHIFT opens/peels architectural boundaries around a central portal.
  Honda uses forward travel, wheel/road continuity, mirror recession and a service-bay
  handoff.
- **Focal subject:** SHIFT's subject is a pair of movable partitions and the space they
  create. Honda's subject is the same selected scooter and the ownership path around it.

### Versus Apex Hybrid

- **Page structure:** Apex scrolls editorial overlays over a persistent retail scene.
  Honda makes product choice and lead actions part of the visual journey, with bilingual
  utility anchored to each decision.
- **Motion grammar:** Apex uses broad camera/environment interpolation. Honda uses a
  directed roadway, product-state cuts and an explicit showroom threshold.
- **Focal subject:** Apex presents a retail/store system. Honda presents one physical
  vehicle and the local people/process that get it on the road.

- **Page structure:** Kinetic uses an exploded product demonstration and later content
  sections. Honda stays with an intact recognizable vehicle and moves through purchase
  and care stages.
- **Motion grammar:** Kinetic separates and reassembles components. Honda advances space,
  lighting and decision layers while the vehicle remains trustworthy and whole.
- **Focal subject:** Kinetic's speaker internals explain construction. Honda's scooter,
  road and branch relationship explain ownership.

## Frontier scene contract — pilot only

The pilot proves roughly 10–15 seconds of intended experience: **street choice → trade
route → showroom threshold**. It does not build the complete catalog, forms, service
booking or production bilingual copy before creative judgment.

### Initial state

- 1440 × 900: authentic scooter in a low three-quarter view, occupying roughly 40% of
  width with wheel contact grounded on a Kaduwela road plane. The route line begins under
  the front wheel and leads toward a distant red Macro threshold.
- Clear HTML: `Your next ride, one road away`, one current-model label, `Talk to Macro`,
  language switch and scene progress. Essential navigation never lives in canvas.
- Light: bright tropical daylight with controlled red reflection, not a dark luxury void.

### Interaction state

- Wheel/scroll progress advances road texture and camera parallax while the scooter stays
  optically stable. At the trade beat, the centre line widens into three labelled steps;
  a mirror layer briefly carries the old ride behind the visitor.
- Model/color selection changes only at a settled choice point. Pointer movement gives
  small lighting response, not constant orbit. Touch uses swipe/scroll with large direct
  chapter buttons.
- The exact finance step text remains generic (`Assess`, `Plan`, `Handover`) until the
  customer confirms terms. No calculator implies approval or a guaranteed payment.

### Resolved state

- The road line crosses a sharp red/white showroom threshold. Real branch imagery or an
  accurately art-directed local environment replaces the road; the selected scooter
  remains continuous and gains one after-sales/genuine-parts proof point.
- Resolve to `Built for the road ahead` with `Talk to Macro`, `Call`, and `Directions`.

### Persists and transforms

The selected scooter, wheel contact, route line, model name and primary action persist.
Road environment, light, old-ride mirror, decision labels and branch background transform.
Do not crossfade unrelated full-screen photos while claiming object continuity.

### Reverse scroll

Every scene property derives from clamped progress. Reversing exits the showroom,
collapses the three trade steps back into the road line, restores the opening camera and
returns the scooter to the exact initial state. Product selection persists until changed
directly; scroll direction does not silently swap models.

### Mobile

390 × 844 uses a tall front-three-quarter crop with the scooter below the headline. The
road route runs vertically beside the wheel and direct chapter controls replace tiny
hotspots. Copy never overlays high-detail vehicle regions without a stable backing.

### Reduced motion

Present three direct still chapters—Choose, Trade, Care—with immediate transitions and
the same content/actions. No simulated camera movement, scrubbed frame sequence or
automatically moving road.

### Fallback

If image sequence/canvas/GPU support fails, use an eager authentic product image, HTML
model/route content, a real branch image and working call/directions actions. The fallback
must remain a strong dealer landing page, not an error notice.

### Performance and loading budget

- Opening critical imagery target: under 900 KB compressed total at the reference laptop
  viewport; final limits depend on the real source assets.
- Frame sequence, if chosen: responsive AVIF/WebP or compact local video with poster;
  measure decoded memory and seek behavior before acceptance. No third-party hosted
  runtime is required.
- Cap canvas/WebGL DPR at 1.5 on desktop and 1.25 on mobile; pause offscreen/hidden work.
  Aim for stable 60 Hz on reference desktop and report actual measurements. An image-only
  route still needs temporal and reduced-motion QA.

## User verdict — creative rejection, 2026-09-15

Do not expand the current pilot into production. It repeats SHIFT's fixed-stage and
scroll-chapter mechanism closely enough to feel like the same creative direction. The
result reads as cards changing while a product image moves, rather than a persuasive
Dio experience at the level suggested by Ciao Energy and Interactive Studio.

User-observed functional failures also supersede the automated pass: on mobile the Dio
color is hidden behind text, and after part of the journey the color controls no longer
work. These remain evidence against this direction; do not spend time patching them
before a new concept is chosen.

The replacement must be pitch-ready in creative scope. Its proof must expose as much of
the eventual experience as necessary to judge arrival, product desire, feature story,
ownership/trust and conversion action on desktop and mobile. Ideas such as ignition,
feature reveals or a self-propelled ride are examples of the intended experiential depth,
not locked implementation instructions. Start again from the Dio buyer and product; use
a new spatial composition and motion grammar, then record a new scene contract and
composition comparison before changing runtime code.

The reference set is a starting floor, including any older award-gallery work, not the
creative ceiling. The replacement should seek a current frontier mechanism and express
Honda Kaduwela's particular product desire, local trust and ownership story strongly
enough to become memorable and shareable. “300 times better” records the user's ambition
for attractiveness and conversion, not a numerical claim to publish without measured
evidence.
